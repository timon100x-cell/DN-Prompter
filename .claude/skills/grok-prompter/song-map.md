# Stage 5A: Song map and shot plan

Goal: before any shot prompt, every second of the song has a planned shot that Tim has approved, and nothing repeats.

Why this step exists (King of Nothing, Nov 2026): Tim does not bring a finished shot list. He works through the song section by section ("what do I do between 1:10 and 2:11?"), and he thinks in song time. Without a map, plans were made before the length or the music of a section was known, so the intro was planned three times, the chorus was switched to night after its prompts were written, and a scene was repeated.

## 1. Take in the song once

Ask for these in one message, at the start of stage 5 (or earlier if Tim offers them):

- The lyrics with a timestamp at the start of each section (intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, solo, bridge, breakdown, outro). Rough times are fine.
- The total length of the song.
- What the music does in each section that has no words: instrumental build, banjo solo, guitar solo, breakdown, quiet break.
- Clip length. Default about 4 seconds; Tim has used 3 seconds for a fast intro and 5 seconds for calm shots.

Save it as the "Song map" table in the bible: section, start, end, seconds, lyric or music, clips (seconds divided by clip length, rounded).

If Tim already has his own shot list, put his shots into the shot table against the song map and only ask about gaps.

## 2. Fix the frame of each section before planning it

For each section, before any shot ideas, settle in one short message (with a recommended answer marked, so "yes" is unambiguous):

- **Time and state:** day, dusk, night, burning, the morning after. Write it in the song map. It stays fixed for the section and is repeated in every prompt of that section.
- **Story or band:** roughly how many clips go to the band. Solos and breakdowns usually go to the soloist cut against story shots.
- **Who is in it:** which characters appear in this section.

## 3. Plan the section, one line per shot

- One line per clip: shot number, time, lyric or music moment, what we see, and whether the picture already exists ("have it") or is new.
- Number shots by section so they stay stable: I1, I2 (intro), V1 (verse 1), C1 (chorus), S1 (solo), BR1 (bridge), F1 (final chorus), O1 (outro). Band shots inside a section keep their section number and say "band" in the line.
- When Tim asks for ideas, give options with one recommendation and say why in one sentence.
- **No repeated setups.** Before showing the plan, check every line against the "Setups used" list in the bible. A setup is a subject in a place doing a thing from a similar camera position (for example "the king on his throne, seen from the front"). A new shot of the same place needs a clearly different subject, action or camera position. If a lyric points back to an earlier setup, offer a different image for it and say which earlier shot it would repeat.
- Count the clips against the song map. If the plan is longer or shorter than the section, say so and offer what to cut or add.

Then stop and wait for Tim's OK. Write no prompts before he approves the plan. Changes he makes go into the plan, and the plan is shown again only if more than two lines changed.

## 4. After approval

- Add the shots to the shot table in the bible (one table for the whole video), and add each new setup to "Setups used".
- Make any plates or story-state overviews the section needs first (see `locations.md`), then the shot prompts (see `shots.md`).
- When Tim says a section is done, mark its shots done in the table.

## When Tim jumps ahead

Tim may ask for a single image outside the plan ("a prompt of angry villagers in the square"). Write it, then add it to the shot table and "Setups used" with its section if he says where it goes, or as "extra" if he doesn't.
