# Stage 1 and 2: World questions and look test

## Stage 1: World questions

Goal: fill the World section of the bible and draft the look line.

How to ask:

- One round at a time, three to five questions per round. Wait for the answers before the next round.
- Give every question two or three concrete suggested answers that fit what Tim has said so far, plus "you choose". Tim answers fastest by picking and tweaking.
- If Tim says "you choose", decide, say what you chose in one line, and move on.
- Skip a question Tim already answered. If he opens with a long description, pull the answers out of it and only ask about the gaps.
- Stay on what will be visible in the images. Do not ask about plot beyond the one-sentence premise. Tim writes his own shot list.

| Round | Ask about | Goes into the bible as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Premise | The video in one sentence. The feeling it should leave. Roughly how many shots. Which saved characters appear (the skeleton cowboy?) and which new ones. Whether this video has band frames (default yes; the details are asked later in stage 4B) | Premise, scope, cast list |
| 2 Place and time | Era, region, season, weather, time of day. One world or several | World facts |
| 3 Light | The main light sources in this world (sun, fire, neon, moon, lamps). Hard or soft. Warm or cool. How bright the darkest scenes may get | Light rules |
| 4 Color | Two or three colors for subjects, and the background colors they sit against. Any color that is reserved for one thing | Palette |
| 5 Camera and film | Film feel (for example 35mm with fine grain, clean digital, 16mm with heavier grain). Favorite framings. How close the camera gets. Low or eye-level | Camera rules, look line |
| 6 Motifs | Objects or images that return through the video. Anything that must never appear | Motifs, never-list |

The look is always a photoreal film still. Do not ask about medium. Ask only about the film feel.

After round 6, write the World section into the bible and show Tim a short summary, not the whole file. Ask for corrections once.

### The look line

One or two fixed sentences that close every prompt of this video, word for word. It holds only: photoreal film still, lens or format feel, grain, sharpness. Example:

`Photoreal 35mm film still with fine grain, natural surface texture, everything in the focal plane sharp.`

Name no materials or scene content in the look line (no "wood", "grass", "skin"). It closes every prompt, including studio character sheets, and named materials get pulled into the image.

Keep light, color and mood out of the look line. Those change per shot and belong in sentences 4 and 5.

## Stage 2: Look test

Goal: lock the look line before any character or location is made, so nothing has to be remade later.

1. Pick one simple scene from this world with no named character: a place, one generic figure or object, the world's main light.
2. Write that same scene three times. Sentences 1 to 5 stay identical. Only the look line differs (for example fine-grain 35mm, heavier-grain 16mm, clean large-format).
3. Tim generates all three at 16:9 and shows them.
4. He picks one. Lock that look line in the bible. Save the pick as `refs/style-pick` and note it in the bible.

If none works, ask what is off in one question, adjust, and run three more. Do not run more than two rounds without changing something concrete.

The style pick is a reference for Tim's eye and for you when checking results. Do not attach it to shot prompts by default, because it would pull its scene content into the shot. Attach it only if a series starts drifting in look, with the job line "Image 3 sets the film look and grain only".
